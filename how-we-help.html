Institutional Knowledge Documentation

The customer relationships are in the owner's phone. The pricing logic is in their head. The vendor dynamics, the exception handling, the twenty years of judgment calls that make the business run — nobody else knows any of it.

We capture all of it. Customer relationship maps, pricing frameworks, operational decision trees, supplier agreements and the informal terms that actually govern them. We put it somewhere it can survive the people who currently hold it.

This is the foundational work. Everything else — automation, succession, valuation — depends on knowing what the business actually knows. It also happens to be the single highest-leverage thing an owner can do before any kind of transition.

What we deliver: A documented knowledge archive structured for operational handoff — not a filing cabinet, but a working resource the next person in the role can actually use on day one.
02

Administrative Task Automation

A $15M company typically has an office manager or operations coordinator spending 15–20 hours a week on work that could be 3–4. Manual invoicing. Chasing approvals through email chains. Pulling weekly reports from three different systems by hand. Scheduling and dispatch phone tag. Purchase order routing. Collections follow-up.

The automation isn't exotic. In most cases it means connecting the tools the business already owns and stopping the copy-paste. We identify which manual processes are candidates, map the current flow, and design the replacement — then document it so it doesn't depend on one person knowing how it works.

The framing matters: this isn't about eliminating roles. It's about returning capacity to the people who already work here. The office manager who spends 4 hours a week on reports instead of 14 can do something more valuable with the other 10. That's the conversation we have with your team.

Typical result: 10–15 hours per week of manual administrative work returned to the business within the first 90 days — without adding headcount or replacing systems.
03

Revenue Leakage Identification

Revenue leakage is money already being earned that isn't being fully captured. It's different from waste — it's not that the business is spending wrong, it's that it's not collecting what it's already owed.

We look at unbilled time and materials, inconsistent pricing across the team, invoices going out late, discounts being offered without policy, contracts expiring without renewal conversations, and jobs closing without final reconciliation. In businesses this size, these add up fast.

Common finding: Pricing inconsistency — the owner charges one rate, the team charges another, and nobody has noticed because the business is busy. Standardizing it and closing the gap often recovers 2–4% of revenue with no new sales required.
04

Process Standardization

Right now, the best people do it one way and everyone else improvises. That inconsistency is both a quality risk and an owner-dependency problem. When the best person is out, quality drops. When the owner is unavailable, decisions stall. The business runs on tribal knowledge and availability, not on systems.

We document how things actually get done — the real process, not the org chart version — then work with your team to standardize what works and simplify what doesn't. The result is a business where a new hire can get up to speed in days instead of months, and where the work doesn't depend on who happens to be in the room.

Why this matters for valuation: A buyer due diligence team will ask to see documented processes. If the answer is "it lives with our people," that's a discount — not a premium.
05

Operational Cost Waste

Five percent waste is conservative for businesses this size. On a $20M company, that's $1 million sitting in the business right now — in redundant vendor contracts, software subscriptions nobody uses, purchasing without negotiation leverage, staffing patterns that don't match actual demand, and process steps that exist because they always have.

We find it, document it, and show you the verified math. We don't recommend cuts without showing the current cost and the alternative. Every finding comes with a number attached.

What we deliver: A prioritized waste register — every identified opportunity with its estimated annual value, confidence level, and what it would take to capture it. No guesses. No round numbers.

We show you what your business is worth today — with the numbers to back it up — and what it could be worth in 18 months with the right work in place. The entry conversation isn't "you should sell." It's "let me show you what this is actually worth, and what it could be."
