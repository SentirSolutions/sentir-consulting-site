Why This Book Exists

We have been here before.

In the early 1800s, the textile workers of northern England watched power looms arrive in their mills. Some smashed the machines. Most were afraid. A few figured out that the question was not whether the machines were coming — they were — but who would learn to run them.

We are in that kind of moment now. And most of what's being written about it is either vendor language, breathless headlines, or fear. This book strips all of that away. It is not about how to buy AI tools. It's about how to think clearly while everyone around you is selling.

It draws on years of delivering technology transformation at enterprise scale — inside federal agencies and large commercial organizations, in the rooms where enterprise AI actually gets built and deployed. Watching what works. Watching what fails. And bringing that pattern recognition to the people who never had access to it.

From the Introduction

"Not engaging with AI is not a neutral choice. The people who recognize it early and act thoughtfully — not frantically — are the ones who come through this kind of transition with their footing intact."

Who It's For

Three audiences, one honest conversation.

01

The business owner

You built something real, and you're being pitched AI daily by people who don't understand your business. This book shows you where the genuine value is — hidden waste, undocumented knowledge, the valuation gap — and how to evaluate anyone who wants to help you.

02

The working person

You've been with a company for ten or twenty years and you're watching AI change your industry. This book is written for you honestly — what is actually happening to jobs, what it means for yours, and what to do before the wave reaches you.

03

The person starting out

You're building a career in a world where the rules are changing mid-game. The industrial revolution needed a generation to run it. So does this one. You are not competing against AI — you are inheriting it.
