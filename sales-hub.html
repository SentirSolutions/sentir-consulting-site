★ Why we win New

The 30-second version of our moat. Internalize this — it's the spine of every call.
  • We've actually been in the room. Tim was a VP at 35 and inside two acquisitions — commercial and federal. We know exactly what a buyer looks for and what kills a valuation. Almost nobody serving local businesses can say that.
  • We're honest about AI. Tim just came from the biggest enterprise AI conference in the country. We know the difference between a demo and a result. We're not selling AI tools — we're the people who cut through the hype.
  • We only get paid when they save. Our model is gain-share — a share of verified first-year savings, paid as they're realized. We don't win unless they win. That disarms the "what's this going to cost me" fear instantly.
  • A piece of the savings goes back to their people — in writing. The workforce reinvestment covenant is in every engagement. We're not here to cut their team; we're here to protect and upskill the people who built the business.
  • We make the business worth more and less dependent on the owner. A business that lives in the owner's head sells at 2–3x earnings. Documented, systematized, owner-independent, it sells at 4–5x. On a typical client that's $400K–$600K of real family wealth we help create.

Sincerity is the product. Not the tools, not the frameworks. The fact that Tim actually gives a damn what happens to the 45-year-old office manager. Owners feel that in the first conversation — and it's what builds the referral practice.

1 Who to call

We screen on a pain, not a size. If a prospect fits this shape, they're worth a call. If they don't, move on — we're picky on purpose.

The profile — a pain, not a revenue box

  • The business grew but the owner didn't get richer doing it. Revenue climbed; profit thinned as overhead ate the growth. The owner runs harder every year for the same reward — and it still runs on them. Don't tollgate on revenue or age — the squeeze is the signal, at any size.
  • Privately held, owner-operated, in business 10+ years — the owner is the business: pricing, customer relationships, how things really work all live in their head.
  • They're being pitched "AI" by vendors and have no way to judge any of it.

The gate we can't manufacture — a compelling event they felt

A plateau without an event is a conversation; a plateau with one is a client. Hunt the event: buyers circling, a soft or insulting offer, a health scare, a partner cashing out, a key person who gave notice, a kid who walked. The test isn't "did something happen" — it's "did they feel the consequence."

Disqualify fast (so Tim only walks into rooms that can close)

  • The comfortable owner going nowhere. Every business has waste and risk — that's need, and need is universal and useless as a qualifier. Comfortable owners don't move. We target want, not need.
  • An immovable gatekeeper who feels threatened and controls the owner. If the right-hand who runs operations has a reason to want us gone and the owner won't overrule them, walk. Ask early: who actually runs it day to day, and do they have a reason to want you gone?

Industries, in priority order

Commercial printing & trade services · logistics/distribution · light manufacturing · professional services firms · commercial construction · family-owned wholesale & supply.

Territory — two corridors out of Charlottesville

  • I-64: Staunton → Waynesboro → Charlottesville → Zion Crossroads/Louisa → Richmond west
  • I-66: Front Royal → Winchester → Warrenton → Gainesville/Haymarket → Manassas

Work warm, but not personal. Reach strangers through pull channels, not friends: CPAs and business bankers who already serve these owners, and trusted referrers who get inbound from owners getting sell-ready. One CPA or banker who trusts us beats fifteen cold drops — they know exactly who just had something happen. Our founding proof point should be a non-friend so the win proves the method, not the relationship.

2 What to say to open the door

The goal of the call is not to explain Sentir. It's to earn 30 minutes of Tim's time. Stay curious, stay short.

The door-opener (works on any owner, any industry)

"Tim works with owners whose business grew but whose profit didn't — it got bigger and somehow harder, and it still runs on them. No charge, no pitch: he'll show you where the profit's leaking and what the business is worth today versus what it could be worth if it ran without you. Most owners are leaving real money on the table and don't know it. Worth a half hour?"

If they ask what it's about

Keep it on value and succession, never on technology:

"It's about two things every owner in this spot cares about: getting more out of the business now, and making sure it's worth what it should be when you step back. Tim finds the waste and the gaps that buyers and bankers care about — and he only gets paid when you actually save."

The credibility line (use it early)

"Tim was a VP at 35, inside two acquisitions — one commercial, one federal. He knows exactly what buyers looked for and what we had to fix to get there. Most businesses that grew the way yours did have the same fixable gaps."

The whole job of the call

Get a yes to a 30-minute meeting with Tim. That's it. Don't sell the engagement, don't quote a price, don't explain the method. Curiosity + a low-risk ask + Tim's track record. Then book it.

3 Handle the wariness New

Our buyers are skeptical of outsiders and especially of "AI." That's healthy — agree with them, then redirect to value. Never argue.
"We're not interested in AI / we're not a tech company."
"Good — neither is Tim, for its own sake. He's allergic to the hype. This isn't about installing software or replacing anybody. It's about what your business is worth and where money's leaking. The 'how' is his problem, not yours."
"We're doing fine / we're not looking to sell."
"Most of Tim's clients aren't selling tomorrow either. But the same things that make a business sellable make it run better and worth more today — documented, less dependent on you. Even if you never sell, you'd want that. Thirty minutes to see what it looks like?"
"We're too small for consultants."
"That's exactly who Tim built this for. The big firms can't afford to care about a business your size. Tim brings the enterprise rigor down to companies that were never going to get it otherwise — and he's paid out of the savings, not up front."
"What's this going to cost me?"
"The look is free. If you engage, Tim's paid a share of what he actually saves you — verified, as it's realized. He doesn't win unless you do. And a slice of the first-year savings goes back into your team, in writing."
"How do I know you understand my business?"
"Fair. That's why the first conversation is Tim listening, not presenting. He's sat across from owners who built something real with their hands and their relationships. You'll know in the first ten minutes whether he gets it."

5 You got a meeting — now what

This is the win. Protect it.
  • Book Tim immediately while the owner's warm — same day if you can. Get it on the calendar before you hang up.
  • Send Tim the brief: who they are, the business, what opened the door, anything personal you learned (kids in the business? thinking about retiring? a buyer sniffing around?). Tim walks in already knowing them.
  • Confirm 24 hours before. A confirmed meeting is a kept meeting.
  • Then get out of the way. Tim runs the room. Your job's done — beautifully.

6 Follow up — the 72-hour rule

You are the oxygen. Tim opens on energy and moves on; you keep the thread alive. This is the whole value you add.
  • Every contact gets a follow-up within 72 hours. No exceptions. A "no thanks" you can retire; silence you keep warm.
  • After a call with no meeting yet: short note + the one-pager + a single soft next step ("Tim's near Staunton on the 12th — worth 30 minutes then?").
  • After a meeting: thank-you + whatever Tim promised to send. Same day.
  • Warm-but-not-ready: tag for a touch every 4–6 weeks. These convert later. Don't drop them.
